Family Resources Survey 2024–25 · UK

What to know first

Around 1 in 4 people in the UK were classed as disabled in 2024–25. That is about 16.7 million people.

Among people aged 16–64, the employment rate was 52.8% for disabled people and 82.5% for non-disabled people. The difference is 29.7 percentage points.

PIP is a benefit, not a count of all disabled people. The 4.1 million PIP figure covers people entitled to PIP in England and Wales.

What does “disabled” mean in these figures?

The Family Resources Survey follows the core Equality Act 2010 definition: a physical or mental impairment that has a substantial and long-term negative effect on normal daily activities. Other sources can use slightly different definitions, so the site keeps the source and coverage with each figure.

How to read the data

Different sources do not always measure disability in exactly the same way. The Family Resources Survey follows the core Equality Act 2010 definition: a physical or mental impairment with a substantial and long-term negative effect on normal daily activities. Labour-market publications use survey classifications aligned with the Equality Act definition.

Each figure keeps its original area and time period. For example, the PIP figure covers England and Wales, while the Family Resources Survey headline figure covers the whole UK.
