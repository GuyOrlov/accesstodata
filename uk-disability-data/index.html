How to use this page

Start with the four headline cards, then open a topic for more detail. Percentages always keep their original population, area and time period beside them.

PIP is a benefit measure, not a count of all disabled people. Survey estimates and administrative benefit counts answer different questions and should not be compared as if they measure the same population.

What does “disabled” mean in these figures?

The Family Resources Survey uses a definition consistent with the core Equality Act 2010 definition. Other sources can use slightly different definitions, so each figure keeps its source and coverage.

What this shows

Reported disability prevalence has increased over the decade for the total population, children and working-age adults.

What this cannot tell us

These changes do not by themselves explain why prevalence changed. Survey methods, population age, awareness, diagnosis and social conditions may all matter.

Survey figures are estimates. Small differences may not be statistically meaningful. Where confidence intervals or standard errors are available, use the source methodology before drawing conclusions.

How to read the data

Use “What this shows” and “What this cannot tell us” below charts. Association does not prove that disability caused an observed difference.

Different sources do not always measure disability in exactly the same way. The Family Resources Survey follows the core Equality Act 2010 definition: a physical or mental impairment with a substantial and long-term negative effect on normal daily activities. Labour-market publications use survey classifications aligned with the Equality Act definition.

Each figure keeps its original area and time period. For example, the PIP figure covers England and Wales, while the Family Resources Survey headline figure covers the whole UK.
